# Connect the completed models

Clustering groups observations under a declared distance and feature representation. Neighbors and Bayes predict supplied labels under different assumptions. A tree exposes split rules; regression predicts a number; a neural network learns a parameterized transformation.

For the completed capstone, identify the fitted representation, candidate-selection rule, baseline, evaluated observations and strongest remaining limitation. Explain how changing a feature, source population or available label could change the question.

## Choose a focused follow-up

- For probability and calibration, revisit the Bayes log-score example, then study why a high model probability can still be poorly calibrated.
- For numerical modeling, revisit least squares and the neural gradient check, then study matrix conditioning and optimization.
- For image models, complete the optional local CNN, including its shape trace, derivative check and grouped evaluation.
- For a new dataset, begin with its source conditions, observation unit and leakage boundary. Reserve the evaluation before trying additional models.

Each follow-up reuses an established course prerequisite. Keep the completed source, environment and report as a reproducible example of how a modeling conclusion was reached.
